Visibility & Line of Sight

Core Visibility Rules (06.01)

  • Line of Sight (LoS): An imaginary straight line, 1 mm wide, drawn from any part of the observing model to any part of the observed model. Ignore other models in the observing model's unit and in the observed model's unit.
  • Model Visible: Any part of the observed model is visible to the observing model (unblocked by intervening terrain or models from other units).
  • Model Fully Visible: Every part of the observed model that is facing the observing model is visible to it (i.e. the only thing blocking visibility to any part of that model is the model itself).
  • Unit Visible: One or more models in the unit are visible to the observing model.
  • Unit Fully Visible: Every model in the unit is fully visible to the observing model (the observing model can see through other models in the observed unit).

Terrain Areas, Categories & Movement

Terrain Areas & Categories (13.01 – 13.05)

Terrain features are placed on a defined boundary (base/mat), individually on the battlefield, or in a group that defines an area boundary. The area inside that boundary (or covered by a standalone feature) is a Terrain Area.

  • Exposed Terrain (13.03): Craters, razorwire, scattered debris. Traversed without hindrance; does not make its terrain area Obscuring or grant Hidden.
  • Light Terrain (13.04): Barricades, low walls, statuary. Traversed without hindrance; makes its terrain area Obscuring and allows eligible models within it to be Hidden.
  • Dense Terrain (13.05): Buildings, ruins, armoured containers, woods. Restricts movement; makes its terrain area Obscuring, allows eligible models within it to be Hidden and Gone to Ground, and has the Solid rule.

Terrain & Movement (13.06)

  • Exposed & Light Features: All models can move horizontally and vertically through Exposed and Light terrain features freely.
  • Dense Features:
    • INFANTRY / BEASTS / SWARM / MOBILE models can move horizontally through Dense terrain features (e.g. through ruin walls).
    • INFANTRY / BEASTS / SWARM models can also move vertically through Dense terrain features (e.g. through floors and ceilings).
    • All Other Models: Can move horizontally through Dense terrain features only if all sections of the feature the model's base moves through are 2" or less in height. For sections taller than 2", they must climb vertically to ascend/descend, cannot move through ceilings/floors, and cannot end on upper levels.
  • Climbing Vertically (>2" Height): A model can climb up or down any terrain feature taller than 2" by counting the vertical distance up and/or down as part of its move. It must be within ½" horizontally of the terrain feature when it starts and finishes climbing.
  • Ending Above Ground Level: Only INFANTRY / BEASTS / SWARM / FLY / MONSTER models can be set up or end a move on a surface above ground level, and only if the model is stable and no part of its base overhangs the outer edge of that surface.
  • Solid Protrusion Restriction (13.06 & 13.11): On a terrain feature with the Solid rule (all Dense features), a model cannot end a move with any part of it protruding through an enclosed part of that feature 3" or less from ground level (not even through doors or windows).

Terrain & Visibility Rules

Benefit of Cover (13.08)

Benefit of Cover Ranged Attack: Worsen BS by 1
Checked per attacking model against the target unit as a whole. Does not apply to melee attacks or weapons with [IGNORES COVER] (24.18).

Each time a ranged attack targets a unit, if every model in that target unit meets one or more of the following conditions, that unit has the Benefit of Cover against that attack:

  1. That model has the INFANTRY / BEASTS / SWARM keyword and is within a terrain area (note: within, not wholly within; any terrain area qualifies).
  2. That model is not fully visible to the attacking model due to one or more intervening terrain features and/or one or more intervening obscuring terrain areas (13.10).

Effect: Each time a ranged attack targets a unit that has the Benefit of Cover against it, worsen the BS characteristic of that attack by 1 (e.g. BS 3+ becomes BS 4+).

Example: Benefit of Cover (When Unit E Makes Ranged Attacks)

  • Unit A: Fully visible to every model in Unit E → Does NOT have Cover.
  • Unit B: Not all models in Unit B are within a terrain area (and they are fully visible) → Does NOT have Cover.
  • Unit C: All models in Unit C (INFANTRY) are within a terrain area → Has Benefit of Cover (-1 BS).
  • Unit D: Not fully visible to Unit E due to an intervening obscuring terrain area → Has Benefit of Cover (-1 BS).

Hidden & Gone to Ground (13.09)

Hidden Detection Range: 15"

A model is Hidden while all of the following apply to it:

  1. That model has the INFANTRY / BEASTS / SWARM keyword and is within a terrain area that contains one or more Light or Dense terrain features.
  2. That model's unit did not make one or more ranged attacks during this turn or during the previous turn (in Turn 1, units count as not having made ranged attacks during the previous turn).

Effect: While a model is Hidden, it can only be visible to enemy models that are within its detection range (default 15").

Gone to Ground -3" Detection Range (12")

A model has Gone to Ground while all of the following apply:

  1. That model is Hidden.
  2. That model is not fully visible to the attacking model due to one or more intervening Dense terrain features.
  3. That model's unit did not make one or more ranged attacks during this turn or during the previous turn (units that shot this or last turn cannot go to ground, even if an ability lets them shoot and remain Hidden).

Effect: While a model has Gone to Ground, subtract 3" from that model's detection range (reducing the default 15" detection range to 12").

Obscuring (13.10)

  • Obscuring Terrain Areas: Any terrain area that contains one or more Light or Dense terrain features is an obscuring terrain area.
  • Blocking Visibility: If every line of sight drawn between two models crosses one or more obscuring terrain areas (excluding obscuring terrain areas that one or both of those models are within), those two models are not visible to each other.

Example: Hidden & Obscuring

  • Units A & D: There is an obscuring terrain area between all models in Units A and D (and neither is within it), so none of them are visible to each other.
  • Units B & C (Hidden): All models in Units B and C are INFANTRY within the Light/Dense terrain area, so they are Hidden (15" detection range).
  • Unit D Looking at B & C: Unit D's models are within the 15" detection range of Unit B's models (but not Unit C's), so Unit B's models are visible to Unit D, while Unit C's models are NOT visible to Unit D.
  • Units B & C Looking at D: Unit D's models are visible to both Units B and C, since Unit D is not within a terrain area (not Hidden) and the terrain area B and C are within is excluded from blocking LoS for Obscuring.

Solid & Plunging Fire (13.11 & 22.05)

  • Solid (13.11):
    • All Dense terrain features have the Solid rule.
    • Line of sight cannot be drawn across any enclosed gap in the surface of a Solid terrain feature that is 3" or less from ground level (e.g. ground-floor doors, windows, bullet holes, or narrow gaps between adjacent features; some missions may adjust this height).
  • Plunging Fire (22.05):
    • Each time a model makes a ranged attack targeting a visible unit containing one or more models on ground level, improve the BS characteristic of that attack by 1 if one or more of the following apply:
      1. The attacking model is on a section of a terrain feature that is 3" or more in height.
      2. The attacking model has the TOWERING keyword and the target unit is within 12".
    • Note (23.03): Plunging Fire has no effect on attacks made by, or targeting, AIRCRAFT units.
